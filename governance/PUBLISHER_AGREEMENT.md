## Agreement

Trusted publishing means your signature counts as a trust signal for consumers
of the commons. The bar for that is higher than ordinary contribution. By
requesting trusted publisher status, the publisher confirms:

1. The publisher has the right to contribute all material it submits to the
   commons.
2. Contributions are released into the commons under Apache-2.0, the same
   terms every other contribution sits under.
3. The publisher will not knowingly submit private, proprietary, trade-secret,
   or license-incompatible code.
4. The publisher reviews AI-generated material before submission and takes
   responsibility for what gets published under their key.
5. The publisher discloses material AI assistance in submissions.
6. The publisher signs trusted entries with the accepted publisher key.
7. The publisher reports incorrect, insecure, compromised, or policy-violating
   entries promptly through the revocation runbook.
8. The publisher understands that bad immutable IDs are handled through signed
   revocation feeds and replacement IDs, never through silent mutation of
   existing entries.
9. The publisher accepts removal from `trust/trust.json` if its key is
   compromised or if it violates the commons code of conduct.

This is not a transfer of ownership. The publisher does not retain control over
how the commons uses, distributes, or composes their contribution — that's
governed by Apache-2.0 and by the commons philosophy in `COMMONS.md`. This
agreement is about accountability for what gets signed with the publisher key,
not about rights retained over published code.

## Maintainer Acceptance

Trusted publisher status is active only after maintainers:

- review this agreement,
- review the publisher application,
- add the publisher key to `trust/trust.json`,
- merge the change through CI.
